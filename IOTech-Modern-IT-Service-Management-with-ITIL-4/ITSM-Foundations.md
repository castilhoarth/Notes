# IT Service Management Foundations

## Key Concepts

* **Service:** A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage the specific costs and risks involved.
* **IT service management (_ITSM_):** The set of organizational capabilities for planning, delivering, supporting, and continually improving IT-enabled services. ITSM applies across different types of organizations and business areas.
* **Product:** A configuration of an organization's resources designed to offer value to a consumer. Products can include goods, access to resources, and service actions, and they are used to create and deliver services.
* **Project:** A temporary effort with a defined beginning and end, undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result. Projects can help introduce or improve services, but a service is operated and consumed continuously.
* **Value:** The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. Value is co-created by the service provider and consumer; the provider does not create it alone.
* **Stakeholders:** People or organizations that have an interest in a service. In ITIL, the customer defines requirements and is accountable for outcomes, the user uses the service, and the sponsor authorizes the budget.
* **Service relationship:** The cooperation between a service provider and a service consumer. It includes service provision, service consumption, and service relationship management.

## Governance vs. Management

* **Governance** evaluates, directs, and monitors the organization. It is concerned with doing the right things and ensuring accountability.
* **Management** plans, builds, delivers, and improves services and resources within the direction set by governance. It is concerned with doing things effectively and consistently.
* **ITIL** is a best-practice framework and body of guidance for ITSM. It is not a prescriptive rule book; organizations adapt its recommendations to their context.

## Related Practices

* ITSM aligns with **Agile** ways of working through iterative delivery, flexibility, collaboration, and frequent feedback.
* ITSM aligns with **DevOps** through collaboration between development and operations, automation, shared responsibility, and fast feedback across the service lifecycle.

## Four Dimensions of Service Management

ITIL recommends considering every service-management decision through four balanced perspectives:

* **Organizations and people:** Roles, responsibilities, skills, culture, and communication.
* **Information and technology:** Data, knowledge, applications, infrastructure, and security.
* **Partners and suppliers:** External parties and the relationships needed to deliver services.
* **Value streams and processes:** The activities, controls, and workflows that turn demand into value.
